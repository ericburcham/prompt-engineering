# Prompt Engineering Framework Guide

## Introduction
This guide teaches you how to write better prompts for Copilot and other AI assistants using a proven framework. Think of it as a recipe for clear communication that helps you get exactly what you need from AI.

---

## The Framework: T.C.R.E.I.

A simple mnemonic:  ***T***hinking ***C***learly ***R***educes ***E***rrors ***I***mmediately.

### Writing Your Prompt (3 Parts):

#### 1. **Task** - What do you want the AI to do?
- Start with a clear action verb
- Be specific about the desired output
- Keep it focused on one main objective

**Examples:**
- "Summarize this article in 3 bullet points"
- "Create a project timeline template"
- "Explain quantum computing to a 10-year-old"

#### 2. **Context** - What background information is needed?
- Explain your situation or use case
- Mention any constraints or requirements
- Include relevant details about your audience

**Examples:**
- "I'm preparing a presentation for executives who aren't technical"
- "This is for a small business with limited budget"
- "I need this to comply with standard accounting practices"

#### 3. **References** - What examples or formats should guide the output?
- Attach or provide links to reference materials such as documents, spreadsheets, or articles
- Provide templates or examples to follow
- Specify formatting preferences
- Mention any standards or styles to match

**Examples:**
- "Format it like a professional email"
- "Use a table with columns for date, task, and owner"
- "Follow academic citation style"

### After You Get the Response (2 Steps):

#### 4. **Evaluate** - Does the response meet your needs?
- Check if it completed the task
- Verify it followed your format
- Assess if it's usable for your purpose
- Identify what's missing or incorrect

**Ask yourself:**
- Did it do what I asked?
- Is the format correct?
- Can I use this as-is, or does it need work?
- What's good about it? What's lacking?

#### 5. **Iterate** - Refine based on what you learned

General guidance:

A simple mnemonic:  ***R***epair ***S***omething ***T***hrough ***I***nnovation.

- Revisit the prompting framework
- Separate prompts into shorter sentences
- Try rephrasing or switching to an alternate task
- Introduce constraints

Other things to try:

- Request specific improvements
- Add missing information
- Adjust format or style
- Clarify any misunderstandings

**Examples of iteration prompts:**
- "Make it more concise"
- "Add specific examples for each point"
- "Adjust the tone to be more conversational"
- "Include the section about budget that was missing"

## Complete Example: Writing a Meeting Agenda

### Step 1-3: Initial Prompt (Task + Context + References)
```
Task: Create a meeting agenda for our quarterly review.

Context: I'm organizing a 2-hour meeting with 10 department heads to review 
Q3 performance and plan for Q4. We have a lot to cover and tend to run over time.

References: Use a standard business agenda format with:
- Time allocations for each item
- Clear objectives for each topic
- Designated speakers
```

### Step 4: Evaluate the Response
After receiving the AI's agenda, you review it:
- ✓ It has time allocations
- ✓ Topics are clear
- ✗ No buffer time between topics
- ✗ Missing preparation notes
- ✗ No clear decision points

### Step 5: Iterate with a Follow-up Prompt
```
Good start, but please:
1. Add a 5-minute buffer between major topics
2. Include a "decisions needed" section for each item
3. Add pre-meeting preparation notes
4. Create a version that can be shared 3 days before the meeting
```

## The Prompt Formula

Your initial prompt only needs three parts:
```
Task: [Action verb] [what you want]

Context: [Your situation] [Key requirements or constraints]

References: [Format preference or example]
```

**Remember:** You don't tell the AI how you'll evaluate it. You do that yourself after seeing the response.

## Practice Examples

### Easy: Email Draft

**Initial Prompt:**
```
Task: Write a thank you email to a job interviewer.

Context: I interviewed yesterday for a marketing position at a tech startup. 
The interviewer mentioned they value creativity and informal culture.

References: Professional but friendly tone, about 150 words.
```

**After Receiving Response, You Evaluate:**
- Is it the right length?
- Does it sound professional yet friendly?
- Does it feel appropriate for a creative startup?

**If Needed, Iterate:**
"Perfect length, but make it a bit more creative and add a specific reference to our discussion about their new product launch."

### Medium: Process Documentation

**Initial Prompt:**
```
Task: Create a step-by-step guide for onboarding new team members.

Context: Small marketing team of 5 people, new hires join every few months. 
Currently nothing is documented and training is inconsistent.

References: Use numbered steps with sub-bullets for details. Include a 
checklist at the end.
```

**Evaluate the Response:**
- Are the steps clear and numbered?
- Is there enough detail?
- Is the checklist comprehensive?
- Would a new hire be able to follow this independently?

**Iterate if Necessary:**
"Add time estimates for each step and include links to where they'd find each tool mentioned."

## Tips for Success

### Writing Good Initial Prompts:
- **Be specific** - "Write a paragraph" vs. "Write a 100-word paragraph"
- **Give examples** - Show what good looks like
- **Set boundaries** - Mention what you DON'T want
- **One task at a time** - Complex requests may need multiple prompts

### Evaluating Effectively:
- Read the entire response before judging
- Check against your original requirements
- Note both what works and what doesn't
- Consider if unclear results mean an unclear prompt

### Iterating Smartly:
- Be specific about changes needed
- Build on what worked
- Don't repeat the entire original prompt
- Add new requirements one at a time

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Putting evaluation criteria in the prompt**
   - Wrong: "Make sure it's good enough for executives"
   - Right: "For presentation to executives"

2. **Vague iteration**
   - Wrong: "Make it better"
   - Right: "Shorten to 100 words and add cost estimates"

3. **Not iterating at all**
   - Many people stop after the first response
   - Iteration often leads to much better results

---

## Remember

The process is:
1. **Write** a clear prompt (Task + Context + References)
2. **Read** the response completely
3. **Evaluate** against your needs
4. **Iterate** with specific improvements
5. **Repeat** until satisfied

Start simple, practice regularly, and save prompts that work well for future use!
